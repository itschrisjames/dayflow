v27 — current

Push it off

  • One button, three answers: later today, tomorrow, next week. Each finds a slot that is genuinely free, and shows you where it would land before you commit.
  • Swipe a task to the right to reach it from anywhere — the mirror of swipe-left-to-delete, and the opposite intent.
  • Moving something four times isn't a moral failure, it's information: DayFlow says so once, and offers Someday.
v26

Working hours, and a day that plans itself

  • Set the hours you actually work, which days, and how much breathing room you want between things.
  • Schedule my day drops every loose inbox task into the first slot it genuinely fits — urgent first, heavy work earlier, never in the past, never overlapping anything already there, and never on top of an event from your calendar.
  • Optionally it happens on capture: switch on Schedule new tasks for me and anything you add gets a time immediately.
  • All of it is a single undo.
v25

Import almost anything

  • Import file now takes a JSON list or a CSV as well as a DayFlow backup — a content calendar, a schedule, anything with titles and dates. It guesses the columns, shows you what it found, and adds rather than replacing.
  • Tasks gained a notes field, so captions, links and context travel with the task and turn up in search.
v24

A landing page, feedback, and supporter keys

  • A page to send people, with install instructions, honest limitations and this changelog.
  • Send feedback in Settings, pre-filled with your build number and screen size — and no task names.
  • Supporter keys, verified offline. Nothing is locked behind one; DayFlow asks once, after three weeks of real use, and never again if you say no.
v23

Timers survive the screen going off

  • A running timer is written to storage the moment it starts, so if iOS discards the app in the background it's picked up mid-flight on the next launch, with the real elapsed time. Habit, chore, task and routine alike.
  • A routine rolls forward through however many steps elapsed while you were away.
  • Anything left running over six hours isn't resumed — it's dropped with an explanation rather than logging a nine-hour session you didn't do.
  • DayFlow now keeps the screen awake while a timer runs. Switch it off in Settings if you'd rather save battery.
v22

Calendar sync, and practice sessions by hand

  • Google Calendar two-way sync. Your events arrive as locked blocks; sending your own blocks out is opt-in and off by default.
  • Apple Calendar via the three routes that actually exist: a Shortcuts bridge that reads today's events, .ics import, and .ics export that Calendar opens directly.
  • Habits gained a hand-entered practice log — day, hours and minutes. Practice you did away from your phone counts the same.
  • Fixed: a swiped-open row let the 5m button steal a tap meant for Delete, and the evening recap could appear over what you were doing.
v21

Reliability and recall

  • Reminders no longer depend on the app being awake at the exact minute; anything recently passed still fires.
  • Backups: persistent-storage request, a local snapshot with restore, and a banner when your last export is over a week old.
  • Recurring tasks, search across everything, and an archive so retiring a habit doesn't destroy its history.
  • Subtasks, effort tags, reduced-motion support, and screen-reader labels throughout.
v20

Twelve changes for ADHD brains, plus a tutorial

  • A 5m start button on every task and an optional first-physical-step field.
  • An ambient time bar that drains toward your next block, and 5-minute warnings before a block starts and ends.
  • Streak grace days — one free miss a week — and "showed up N of the last 30 days".
  • Focus 3, automatic Someday parking, carry-forward, momentum queue, evening recap, multi-line paste, and a visible undo bar.
  • A seven-card tutorial on first run, skippable from the first frame.
v19 and earlier

The foundations

  • Today, Week, Habits, Routines, Chore timer, Assistant and Stats.
  • Triple alarms through the Clock app, routine timers, habit practice records, voice capture, eight accent colours, and JSON export/import.
  • A long fight with service-worker caching so that "Check for update" tells the truth.
